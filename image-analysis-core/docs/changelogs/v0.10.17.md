# v0.10.17 — Strip-level radiometric normalisation

## Problem

After the GPS feathering fix (v0.10.15), multispectral band composites and
vegetation-index outputs (GNDVI, NDVI, NDRE) still showed systematic horizontal
banding aligned with the drone's lawnmower flight strips.  The banding was caused
by BRDF effects and DLS-calibration drift between passes: adjacent strips are
captured minutes apart at slightly different sun–sensor angles, giving each strip
a systematically different mean reflectance.  Feathering smooths the pixel-level
seam at overlap boundaries but cannot remove the reflectance-level offset between
strip interiors.

## Fix — `_compute_strip_gains` + two-pass `_gps_composite_band`

`ms_orthorectification.py` now processes GPS composites in two passes:

**Pass 1** — each image is GPS-warped to the DSM UTM grid (as before).  Its
mean valid-pixel reflectance and GPS coordinates are recorded.  The warped TIF
is kept on disk.

**Between passes** — the new `_compute_strip_gains` function groups images into
flight strips:
1. GPS lat/lon coordinates are converted to approximate metres.
2. PCA finds the primary flight axis; the perpendicular (cross-track) direction
   separates strips.
3. Images are projected onto this cross-track axis and sorted.
4. Strip boundaries are detected where the gap between consecutive sorted
   projections exceeds 3× the median intra-strip gap (and is ≥ 5 m).
5. Each strip's mean reflectance is computed as the average of the per-image
   means within that strip.
6. Per-image gain factors = `global_strip_median / strip_mean` are returned;
   all images in the same strip receive the same gain.

**Pass 2** — each warped TIF is reloaded, scaled by its strip gain, and
accumulated with distance-weighted feathering as before.

Because the gain is constant within a strip, no new radiometric boundary is
introduced at individual image edges — only the inter-strip reflectance offset
is removed.
